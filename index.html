SyPAS: Synthetic Socket Perception and Visual Servoing for Robotic Connector Insertion

Abstract

Nearly all robotic assembly tasks involve the basic primitive of inserting one workpiece into another, known in its simplest form as the peg-in-hole problem. More complex derivatives of this problem, such as electrical connector insertion, require visual localization that remains robust to positional uncertainty, non-circular geometry, and nearby distractors. Several methods have been explored for providing this information to the robot, but many rely on task/component-specific labeling at one extreme or cost significant inference time at the other. This paper shows that insertion-grade socket localization can be learned using only procedural synthetic data, without any real training images or manual annotations. We present SyPAS, a perception and visual servoing pipeline in which hole-like synthetic training images are generated to train a heatmap-regression model for initial socket localization. The predicted centerpoint is refined with Segment Anything Model 2, and optical-flow tracking provides low-latency updates during robot motion for closed-loop alignment prior to compliant insertion. Across real images of HDMI, RJ45, BNC, and USB-A sockets, none of which were seen during training, the perception pipeline achieves success rates above 98%. Robot experiments with compliance control result in insertion success rates of 100% on HDMI trials and 91% on USB-A trials (n=150 each), with mean servoing times under 5 seconds and final servoing errors of (1.0 mm, 0.6 degrees) and (1.2 mm, 1.1 degrees) respectively. These results demonstrate that procedural synthetic training can generalize to unseen real-world connector insertion without an a priori dataset or hand labeling.
